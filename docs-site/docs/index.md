# Nexus Scholar User Guide

Nexus Scholar helps research teams manage an evidence-review workflow from the
first protocol draft through full-text eligibility decisions.

This guide is written for students, research assistants, lab managers, and
principal investigators. It explains what to click, what to check, and what a
team should record as audit evidence. You do not need to know how the
application is built to follow it.

<div class="hero-panel" markdown>

The walkthrough uses a real scientific example: digital health interventions
for cardiometabolic risk management in primary care. The records shown in the
screenshots came from live provider searches run on May 31, 2026. The tutorial
decisions are training decisions for learning the product, not a completed
systematic review.

</div>

## What The Current App Covers

<div class="workflow-grid" markdown>

<div class="workflow-card" markdown>
**1. Protocol**

Create the project, question, eligibility criteria, provider policy, reviewer
policy, and full-text policy.
</div>

<div class="workflow-card" markdown>
**2. Search**

Run provider searches in the background and inspect raw and unique result
counts.
</div>

<div class="workflow-card" markdown>
**3. Corpus**

Review search results, metadata quality, provider provenance, filters, columns,
and record detail.
</div>

<div class="workflow-card" markdown>
**4. Lock**

Deduplicate records and freeze the representative corpus before screening.
</div>

<div class="workflow-card" markdown>
**5. Screen**

Assign reviewers, record title and abstract decisions, and resolve
disagreements when they occur.
</div>

<div class="workflow-card" markdown>
**6. Full Text**

Retrieve legal open-access artifacts, audit failures, screen full text, and
record final eligibility outcomes.
</div>

</div>

Planned workflows such as extraction, risk-of-bias appraisal, synthesis,
exports, quotas, and production billing are outside this tutorial.

## Scientific Boundary

Nexus Scholar can organize evidence, preserve audit trails, and make review
work easier to inspect. It does not make a weak search strategy scientifically
valid, and it does not turn training decisions into publishable conclusions.

For an actual review, your team should register or approve the protocol,
validate the search strategy, involve the right subject experts, and retain the
full search and screening audit trail.
