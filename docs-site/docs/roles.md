# Team Roles

Nexus Scholar separates workspace access from project decisions. This helps a
lab invite people broadly without giving every member permission to change a
review.

## Workspace Roles

| Role | Typical person | What they do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Owner | Principal investigator or lab lead | Owns the workspace, manages members, creates projects, and can administer review workflows. |
| Admin | Lab manager, senior research associate, or co-lead | Helps manage the workspace and can administer projects through workspace policy. |
| Member | Reviewer, student, collaborator | Participates in assigned project work. |

## Project Roles

| Role | Typical person | What they do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Owner | Review lead | Controls the project, protocol, corpus lock, setup actions, and workflow handoffs. |
| Reviewer | Screener or extractor | Completes assigned screening work and records rationales. |
| Adjudicator | Senior reviewer or methodology lead | Resolves conflicts with an audit reason. |
| Viewer | Supervisor, student observer, or stakeholder | Reads progress without changing decisions. |

## Operator

The operator is not a research-team role. Operators manage platform-level
states such as disabled users and suspended workspaces. Normal research teams
should not need operator access during a review.

## Practical Advice

- Decide who can adjudicate before screening starts.
- Give reviewer access only to people who should make scientific decisions.
- Use viewer access for supervisors or students who need to follow progress.
- Keep audit reasons specific and professional. They become part of the review
  history.
